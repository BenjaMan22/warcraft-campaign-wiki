Species of the Sourcebook

The D&D World of Warcraft Sourcebook offers 16 species — 11 new, plus WoW-mapping rules for 5 that already exist in the 2024 Player’s Handbook. This page is the verdict on each, measured against the canon boundary.

The short version

All eight launch species are covered by the book. Nothing you need is missing. Eight other species are on offer and seven of them are post-2004.

I will allow one or two exotic species across the whole party, by conversation, at session zero. Not one or two each. See Playable Species at Launch.

The Other Eight — Verdicts

Ranked by how little damage they do to a 2004 campaign.

✅ Worgen — the best exception

In the world at launch: yes. This is the one people forget. Worgen are already loose in Year 29 — Archmage Arugal summoned them from the Emerald Dream to fight the Scourge in Silverpine Forest, lost control, and they have spread through Shadowfang Keep and into Duskwood. The Nightbane pack in Duskwood is a live quest chain at launch.

What does not exist yet is Gilneas as a playable homeland. The Greymane Wall is sealed, the nation is silent, and the Gilnean worgen curse story is a Cataclysm plot six years out.

The version I would run

A victim, not a nation. Someone from Silverpine or Duskwood who was bitten by Arugal’s pack and did not fully turn. Horror, not heritage. No Gilneas, no Greymane, no worgen society — you are the only one you have met who kept their mind.

That character has a natural enemy (Archmage Arugal), a natural fear (the next full moon), and a reason to travel. It is the strongest exotic pick on this list.

Faction: neither. A worgen PC at launch is an outcast from both, which is a feature.

✅ Pandaren — charming, low damage

In the world at launch: barely. Chen Stormstout was a Warcraft III hero unit and that is the complete canon. Pandaria is behind the mist and will be for eight years.

But “a wandering brewmaster from a land nobody has heard of” costs the setting almost nothing, because nobody in Azeroth can contradict them. The mystery is the character.

The version I would run

One traveller, no homeland on any map, no Shado-Pan, no Celestials. When asked where Pandaria is, the honest answer is “I could not find it again if I tried.”

The Monk is open now, so a pandaren brewmaster works exactly as a player would expect. Note that Brewmaster specifically needs the WoW sourcebook — see Monk.

Faction: neutral, which suits a party leaning one way and wanting a bridge.

⚠️ Mechagnome — needs a reskin, then it works

In the world at launch: no, but the parts are. Mechagon is a Battle for Azeroth zone from 2019. However — Gnomeregan fell to radiation four years ago, gnomish prosthetics and clockwork limbs are everywhere in Ironforge’s Tinkertown, and Sicco Thermaplugg has been doing things to survivors that nobody has catalogued.

The version I would run

A Gnomeregan survivor whose irradiated body was rebuilt in mechanical parts — by choice, by necessity, or by someone else’s decision. Use the mechagnome stat block, delete every reference to Mechagon and King Mechagon, and call it what it is: a gnome who was repaired.

Faction: Alliance, Gnomeregan Exiles.

⚠️ Draenei — possible, as a castaway

In the world at launch: no. The Exodar crashes on Azuremyst in 2007. The only draenei on Azeroth in Year 29 are the Lost Ones — degenerate, hostile, and living in the Swamp of Sorrows and Blasted Lands near the Dark Portal.

The playable draenei required a full retcon of what “draenei” meant in 2004. Importing one means importing the retcon.

The version I would run

A single uncorrupted exile who came through the Dark Portal with the Horde’s invasion and has been hiding in the Blasted Lands for fifteen years. No Exodar, no Prophet Velen, no naaru — one person from a world that the orcs destroyed, who recognises every orc they meet.

That is a strong character with a real relationship to Draenor’s fall. It is also a lot of retcon to carry.

Faction: would be Alliance, but arrives with no ties to anyone.

⚠️ Earthen — mechanically fine, narratively a landmine

In the world at launch: yes, asleep. Earthen are titan-forged and they are in Uldaman right now, in stasis, at level 35ish. The species itself arrives playable with The War Within in 2024.

The problem is not canon. The problem is that an earthen PC would put a mid-campaign revelation on the character sheet at session zero.

Faction: Alliance by default, but an earthen predates the concept of factions and would find them baffling.

❌ Vulpera — no

Battle for Azeroth, 2018. Vol’dun does not exist, Zandalar is closed, and there is no vulpera presence anywhere in the Eastern Kingdoms or Kalimdor in 2004.

If someone loves the concept

Reskin as a small desert-nomad people out of Tanaris with Steamwheedle Cartel ties, mechanically identical, called something else. That is homebrew rather than import, and I would rather do it honestly.

❌ Dracthyr — no, and the Evoker goes with them

Dragonflight, 2022. Asleep in the Forbidden Reach since before The Second War, created by Neltharion, unknown to everyone including the other Aspects.

A dracthyr PC hands the party a walking piece of Neltharion’s laboratory at level one, and The Dragon in the Court depends on the Black Dragonflight’s works staying hidden.

Both are cut

Dracthyr are not available, and neither is the Evoker. The two are locked together in WoW and I am keeping them locked together here. This is the one entry on this page with no exception route.

A player who wants dragon magic takes Sorcerer (Draconic Sorcery) with a Red Dragonflight or Blue Dragonflight blessing in their bloodline. Same mechanics, no spoiled secret, and it plugs into Alexstrasza and Malygos instead of a laboratory nobody should know about. See Classes of Azeroth.

❌ Haranir — no

Midnight, 2026. From the Underhold beneath Khaz Algar, a continent that has not been discovered. There is not enough published lore for me to reskin it into 2004 honestly, and no in-world hook to hang it on.

Renown

The sourcebook ties species to a starting Renown Score with one of its 20+ factions, built on the Dungeon Master’s Guide renown rules. That is close to what Reputation as a Mechanic already does here.

How I am handling the overlap

My eight-rank reputation ladder came first and is tuned to this campaign’s factions. When the book ships I will map its renown values onto my ladder rather than run two systems. Logged in D&D World of Warcraft Sourcebook.

The species-grants-starting-renown idea is good and I am keeping it: a Darkspear troll should not start at zero with the Darkspear Tribe.
