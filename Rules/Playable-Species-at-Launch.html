Playable Species at Launch

Eight, and only eight. Four to a side.

"Species", not "race"

The 2024 revision of D&D renamed race to species, and split ability score bonuses out of it entirely — those now come from your background. So a Tauren is not stronger than a gnome on the character sheet; a tauren warrior is stronger than a gnome scholar, because of what they did with their life.

This is a better fit for Azeroth than the old rules were. Nothing about being orcish makes you good at fighting. Being raised in Durotar in Year 29 does.

Mapping to 5.5E

All eight are covered by the D&D World of Warcraft Sourcebook — five as remapped Player’s Handbook species, and Forsaken, Tauren and troll as new ones.

Use the book's versions

Character creation happens after the sourcebook ships, so the official species are the ones we use. No stand-ins, no homebrew stat blocks, no “take Human and add these three traits.”

What I do not know yet

Whether the book’s Forsaken carry an undead trait package the way the game does — no eating or breathing, poison and disease immunity, and the Light healing them at reduced effect. Whether troll Regeneration made it in at all. Whether species grant a starting Renown Score and how that lands against Reputation as a Mechanic.

I get early access on 3 November. Anything surprising goes in the reconciliation table on D&D World of Warcraft Sourcebook.

❌ Not Available

These do not exist as options in 2004

Blood elves and draenei arrive with The Burning Crusade (2007). Blood elves exist in lore — Kael’thas Sunstrider leads them through the Dark Portal — but they are offscreen and not Horde. Draenei as presented in TBC required a full retcon of what “draenei” even meant in 2004; the only draenei on Azeroth at launch are the degenerate Lost Ones in the Swamp of Sorrows.

Worgen, goblins, pandaren, dracthyr, earthen, mechagnomes, nightborne, void elves, vulpera, haranir — all post-launch as playable species. Note this is a species list, and it mostly does not gate classes: the Monk is open without pandaren being available. The one place the two are locked together is dracthyr and the Evoker, and both are cut. Goblins exist as a people and make excellent NPCs and, arguably, neutral-party PCs. Worgen exist as Archmage Arugal’s monsters. Pandaren appear as a Warcraft III easter egg only.

The Exotic Exception

Decided

One or two exotic species across the whole party. Not one or two each — one or two total, agreed at session zero, by conversation rather than first-come.

The D&D World of Warcraft Sourcebook adds eight species beyond our eight, and some of them are more defensible in 2004 than people assume. Species of the Sourcebook has the verdict on every one, ranked.

The short version, best to worst for a Year 29 campaign:

SpeciesWhy
1WorgenAlready loose in Silverpine Forest and Duskwood. Play a victim of Archmage Arugal, not a Gilnean. Costs the setting nothing.
2PandarenOne traveller from a place nobody can find. No homeland on the map, no contradiction possible.
3MechagnomeReskin as a Gnomeregan survivor rebuilt in mechanical parts. Delete Mechagon entirely.
4DraeneiOnly as a single castaway who came through the Dark Portal fifteen years ago. Heavy retcon.
5EarthenCanon-legal, but it costs the campaign a reveal I want to keep. Ask me; I will probably say no.
—Vulpera, dracthyr, haranirNo. Nothing to hang them on in 2004.

Why an exception at all

Because a player who is excited about a character will play that character better than one who settled. The canon boundary exists to make the world feel like 2004, not to win an argument.

An exotic PC should be the only one of their kind anyone in the party has met. That is the price of admission and it is also the best thing about it.

High Elves — A Narrow Exception

A handful of High Elves remain Alliance-loyal at launch and are visible in game — Allerian Stronghold’s forebears, the Silvermoon delegation in Stormwind City, Dalaran survivors in the Kirin Tor. Not a playable option in 2004, but the most defensible import if a player wants one, and it does not count against the exotic budget above.

Use Elf (High Elf lineage) from the 2024 PHB. Prestidigitation, Misty Step, and a levelled spell — mechanically the most magical of the elf lineages, which is exactly right.

My ruling

A high elf PC is fine. A blood elf PC is not — the sin’dorei are through the Portal with Kael’thas Sunstrider and their arc has not happened yet.

One exception, and only one

A blood elf Demon Hunter. Illidan’s first sin’dorei intake is the only demon hunter recruitment happening on any world in 2004, so that character has to be a blood elf — there is no other way to build them.

This does not open blood elves generally. There are no blood elf mages, rogues, or warriors, Quel’Thalas stays a ruin offscreen, and Silvermoon does not go on the map. One character, one route, came back alone. See Demon Hunter.

Choosing a Class To Go With It

Each species suits some classes for reasons rooted in its history, not its stat block. Classes of Azeroth is the hub, with a page for every one of the thirteen — what the class means in Year 29, what a level 1 of it actually looks like, every WoW spec mapped to a 2024 subclass, and specific species pairings worth stealing.

The short version

Orc shaman — reviving a tradition the Legion destroyed. Tauren druid — first generation, no elders. Forsaken anything — you remember your living career. Night elf druid — asleep for the whole Third War. Gnome rogue — trained in a city that no longer exists. Forsaken priest — the Light burns you and you use it anyway. Troll hunter — the raptor is not tame, and that is understood by both of you.
