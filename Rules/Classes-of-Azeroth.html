Classes of Azeroth

The hub. Twelve of the thirteen WoW classes are playable. Each has its own page with lore, level-1 identity, every specialization graded against a 2024 subclass, and species pairings worth stealing.

Decided

All classes are open except the Evoker. The faction and species locks stay. Those rules do different jobs — the class list is about what a player can be, the locks are about what Azeroth in Year 29 can explain.

The three post-vanilla classes are open as rarities: you are not joining an order, you are one of a handful. Each page gives concrete origins to pick from at session zero.

Flavour first, mechanics second

Where a WoW spec has no clean 5.5E equivalent, the flavour is the thing worth preserving and the rotation is not. A Protection Warrior is the one who steps in front and shouts; whether the system has a taunt button is a separate and less interesting question.

The gradings below exist to set expectations honestly at session zero — not to talk anyone out of a character. Nothing on this page is a “don’t play this”.

Not available

Evoker is cut

The class is species-locked to the dracthyr, and dracthyr are Neltharion’s hidden experiment — a PC hands the table a piece of his laboratory at level one and The Dragon in the Court depends on that staying buried.

It is also the worst-supported class in 5.5E: Devastation converts cleanly and Preservation and Augmentation have no equivalent at all, which the WoW sourcebook does not fix. Cutting it costs one spec that worked and removes two that never would.

A player who wants dragon magic takes Sorcerer (Draconic Sorcery) with a Red Dragonflight or Blue Dragonflight blessing in their bloodline. Same mechanics, no spoiled secret, and it plugs into Alexstrasza and Malygos.

Three rulings worth reading before session zero

Death knights are not Ebon Blade — that order is founded four years from now. You broke free of the Scourge on your own, and Death Knight gives three ways that could have happened.

Demon hunters are night elf or blood elf. There are three specific routes in and one of them is homebrew. See Demon Hunter — and note the blood elf route is a deliberate, narrow exception to the no-blood-elf rule.

Monks need a local tradition rather than a monastery. Kaldorei Warden discipline, tauren wrestling, troll fighting-forms. Do not say “chi” at the table.

One subclass you own but cannot use

Reanimator is in Ravenloft, but it is an Artificer subclass — and the Artificer is not in the 2024 Player’s Handbook. It lives only in Eberron: Forge of the Artificer.

So Reanimator, and the whole Artificer chassis, are off the table unless that book gets bought. Worth knowing before a player finds it in Ravenloft and asks. No WoW class maps to Artificer anyway — it is the gnomish-engineer build, not a spec conversion.

The Four Systemic Differences

These are not problems to solve

Four things WoW does that 5.5E structurally does not. Knowing them stops you trying to convert something that was never going to convert, and tells you what to say at session zero.

1 · No threat, no aggro

Affects: Protection Warrior · Protection Paladin · Guardian Druid · Blood DK · Brewmaster Monk

Nothing in 5.5E makes an enemy attack you. 5e assumes positioning and the Sentinel feat do the work that threat does in WoW, and the WoW sourcebook adds nothing here either.

What to do: play the flavour. Stepping in front, shouting, and refusing to move are all roleplay, and Banneret (HoF, owned) makes the shouting mechanical. Nobody at the table will miss a threat meter.

2 · No stacking timers

Affects: Affliction Warlock · Restoration Druid · Restoration Shaman

WoW is built on applying and maintaining damage- and healing-over-time. 5.5E has perhaps three such spells and no management game around them. Healing here is burst and reactive rather than pre-emptive.

What to do: say it at session zero. An Affliction warlock who curses people and watches them rot plays fine as a character; they just resolve their damage immediately. Undead Patron (RHW, owned) gets closer to the decay theme than Fiend does.

3 · One pet, not six

Affects: Demonology Warlock

Pact of the Chain gives you a single familiar. 5e limits summoning deliberately — six pets is six extra turns and the table crawls.

What to do: your imp is the whole pact, and it gets nastier as you level. If the player wants a horde, they want a different game, and steering them to Destruction is kinder than homebrewing it.

4 · No resource meters

Affects: every class, slightly

Rage, combo points, runes, chi, and mana-as-a-bar all refresh per-second; 5e refreshes per-rest. There is no equivalent and building one makes the conversion worse, not better. Reflavour freely and do not build a subsystem.

What the Sourcebook Adds

The shape of the list is informative

6 new subclasses — Oath of the Ebon Blade, Oath of the Holy, Demon Hunter (Ranger), Shadow Domain, Discipline Domain, Path of the Elements. 3 updated — Swashbuckler (Rogue), Path of the Spiritual Guardian (Barbarian), Warrior of Mystic Brews (Monk). 13 class skins — reflavouring guidance for every WoW class.

WotC built new subclasses only where the 2024 PHB had a genuine hole — death magic, absorb-healing, void damage, fel mobility, elemental weapons. What they did not fix is threat, stacking timers, and mass summoning, which is exactly the list above. Those are design differences, not oversights.

The classes with no WoW equivalent

Bard has no WoW class. The nearest thing Azeroth has is a troll drummer or a Tauren storyteller — a background, not a class conflict. College of Spirits (RHW, owned) is an excellent troll voodoo build, and College of the Moon (HoF, owned) suits a priestess of Elune better than any cleric domain does.

Artificer would be the gnomish engineer, and it is not available — the class lives only in Eberron: Forge of the Artificer, which I do not own. See the books table above.

What Your Class Means Here

The thing every class has in common in Year 29

The people who would have trained you properly are dead. The Silver Hand is leaderless, Dalaran is a crater, orcish shamanism was nearly lost to the Legion, Gnomeregan is sealed, and half the Cenarion Circle is still asleep. You are not the inheritor of a proud unbroken tradition. You are what’s left, taught in a hurry by someone who was themselves taught in a hurry.

At level 1 you are competent at one thing and untested at everything else. Nobody has heard of you. That is the point — this campaign starts in a village with a local problem, and the first person who trusts you will do so because you were standing there.

If a Player Just Wants It To Work

The five smoothest conversions

Every one of these is buildable from the core Player’s Handbook today, with no gaps and nothing to buy.

Fire Mage (Wizard, Evoker) · Retribution Paladin (Oath of Vengeance) · Feral or Balance Druid (Circle of the Moon / Stars) · Beast Mastery Hunter (Beast Master) · Destruction Warlock (Fiend + Agonizing Blast)

And the two the mechanics will not carry: Affliction Warlock and Demonology Warlock. Both are still playable on flavour — see Warlock — but tell the player before they build, not after.
