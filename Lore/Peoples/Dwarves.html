Dwarves

In a sentence

Miners, drinkers, and unexpectedly the species with the most disturbing origin story on the continent.

Playable: Yes · Faction: Alliance · Homeland: Dun Morogh · Capital: Ironforge · Leader: King Magni Bronzebeard

Where They Stand

Ironforge is the Alliance’s most functional institution — a mountain-city under Dun Morogh that has fed, armed, and sheltered every ally who asked. It currently shelters the entire gnomish nation in Tinker Town, and does not complain about it.

The Three Hammers

Dwarven unity ended in a civil war three centuries ago. Three clans, three claims:

ClanWhereStatus
BronzebeardIronforgeWon. Rule the mountain.
Wildhammer ClanAerie Peak, The HinterlandsExiled north. Gryphon-riders. Alliance-friendly but not Alliance-subject.
Dark Iron DwarvesBlackrock MountainExiled, summoned Ragnaros, now enslaved to him. Enemies.

The war began over the succession when the old king died leaving three sons. It ended with two clans driven out and a grudge that has now outlived everyone who started it. Nobody living remembers the actual argument. Everybody remembers the outcome.

How They Got Here

They woke up in Uldaman. Or that is the oldest story — that the dwarves came out of the deep places and built Ironforge, and that there were mechanisms down there they did not build.

They kept to the mountain for most of recorded history, trading iron and steel and staying out of human wars.

The Three Hammers broke them three centuries ago and scattered two thirds of the people.

The Second War brought them out. Dwarven steel armed the Alliance, dwarven gryphon-riders — Wildhammers, notably, not Bronzebeards — fought in the sky, and Ironforge discovered it liked being useful.

Then archaeology became a national obsession. The Explorers’ League formed to answer one question: where did we come from? They have been digging ever since, and the answers coming out of Uldaman are not what anybody expected.

Who Leads Them

King Magni Bronzebeard — a working king. He is in the forge most days, he knows his smiths by name, and he is the most personally liked head of state in the Alliance.

Under him sits a council of thanes and guildmasters, and it has real teeth. A dwarven king who ignores his guilds finds the work slows down.

Two institutions matter as much as the crown:

  • The Explorers’ League — funds expeditions worldwide, answers to nobody in practice, and is currently the most influential body in Ironforge
  • The Senate — the clan assembly, slow and procedural and where the Wildhammer question keeps failing to get resolved

Clan comes before crown. A Wildhammer’s first duty is to Aerie Peak, and Magni knows better than to test it.

Where They Fit

They pay for things. Ironforge’s mines and forges make it the only Alliance member with a functioning treasury, and a great deal of what looks like Alliance solidarity is actually dwarven money quietly covering a shortfall in Stormwind.

They also house the gnomes, arm the humans, and are the only Alliance people the night elves find genuinely easy to deal with — dwarves want to dig things up and kaldorei want things left buried, but at least the argument is honest.

What They Just Survived

  • The Third War — Ironforge sent troops north and got fewer back
  • The fall of Gnomeregan, next door, which put an entire refugee nation inside their mountain overnight
  • Three centuries of the Three Hammers, which is still the defining fact of dwarven politics

What They Want

Magni wants his people whole. Reconciliation with the Wildhammers is achievable and he is working on it quietly. The Dark Iron are past that, and he has stopped pretending otherwise.

The Explorers’ League wants the truth about where dwarves came from, and is going to get it whether or not anyone is ready.

Ordinary dwarves want the work to go well and the ale to hold out — and, increasingly, want somebody to explain what the League keeps finding.

Character Traits

  • Archaeological obsession — the Explorers’ League digs everywhere, invited or not
  • Clan loyalty over national loyalty — a Wildhammer’s first duty is to Aerie Peak
  • Hard-drinking, hard-working, genuinely warm — dwarves like people
  • Devout — dwarven paladins are pragmatic rather than pious

How They Talk

Blunt, warm, and full of stone and craft metaphors. Dwarves swear casually and apologise rarely.

PhraseWhen
”By me beard.”Surprise. Also “beard and hammer” for something serious.
”Stone remembers.”Said of grudges, oaths, and history. The nearest thing dwarves have to scripture.
”To the deep.”A toast. Answered with “and back up again” — because not everyone does.
”Ye’ll no’ dig it up wi’ yer hands.”Do the job properly, or bring the right tools
”Mind yer head.”Practical caution, and a running joke about how everyone else is built wrong
”That’s a Bronzebeard answer.”A ruling you dislike but will accept
”Khaz Modan endures.”The formal one. Said at funerals and when signing things.

Playing One

Dwarf PCs get built-in purpose: the Explorers’ League will fund an expedition anywhere, which solves the “why is this character travelling” problem in one sentence.

Four shapes worth taking:

  • Bronzebeard regular — Ironforge-born, mountain-proud, and quietly bothered by what the League keeps hauling home
  • Wildhammer — gryphon country, shaman-friendly, and only conditionally Alliance. Your loyalty runs to Aerie Peak first and you will say so.
  • Explorers’ League field hand — you carry the shovels and the crossbow, and you have seen three things you have not reported
  • Dark Iron defector — hard, and needs real justification. You left Blackrock Mountain and nobody on either side trusts you. Excellent if the player wants a hill to climb.

Questions worth answering at session zero: Which clan, and what does your family think of the other two? What have you dug up that you did not hand in? Do you drink, and is it a problem?
